Design Challenge Prompts: 50 Ideas for Practice

Sorted by category and difficulty, each one ready to open on the board with a timer

Fifty design challenge prompts for practice, sorted by category and difficulty. Each one is a short, open-ended brief of the kind you get in a design or product interview: design something, for a specific product, to help a specific group of people.

Every prompt has a Start link. It opens the prompt on a free online whiteboard with the brief already on the board and a timer ready to go. There's nothing to sign up for.

How to Use These Prompts

  1. Pick a level that stretches you a little. Beginner prompts are a single screen or feature. Advanced ones are systems with competing needs.
  2. Set a time box and stick to it. We suggest 15 minutes for beginner, 20 for intermediate and 30 for advanced. The Start links use those times, and you can change them on the board.
  3. Clarify before you draw. Write down who the user is, what they're trying to do and how you'll know it worked.
  4. Talk out loud. Even alone. In an interview, your reasoning is what gets scored.
  5. Stop and sum up. Leave a minute at the end to say what you designed and what you'd test next.
