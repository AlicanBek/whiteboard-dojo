How to Wireframe for Free in Your Browser

Frames, boxes and arrows: a three-screen flow from start to export

A wireframe is a rough plan of a screen. It shows what goes where and how people move from one screen to the next, before anyone worries about colors or fonts. You don't need a design tool or a paid plan to make one. A free online whiteboard does the job well, and it's often better, because it keeps you from polishing too early.

This guide walks through a small three-screen flow (home, product page, checkout) using the Whiteboard Dojo board. It runs in your browser and doesn't need an account.

1. Start a new board

Open the whiteboard, then open the menu and choose New board. Click the board name in the top right to rename it so you can find it later. Everything saves as you go, so there's no save button to remember.

2. Give each screen its own frame

Press F and drag out a rectangle the size of a phone or a browser window. That's a frame. Anything you draw inside it belongs to it, so when you move the frame, the whole screen moves with it. Double-click the frame's name to call it something useful, like "Home" or "Checkout".

Make one frame per screen and line them up from left to right in the order people will see them.

3. Block out the layout with boxes

Press R for rectangles and block in the big pieces first: the header, the main image, the content, the main button. Keep it gray. A few conventions help anyone reading your wireframe:

  • A box with an X through it means an image. Draw two lines with L from corner to corner.
  • Gray bars mean text. Short and thick for headings, long and thin for body copy.
  • Save color for one thing: the main action on each screen. If everything is colorful, nothing stands out.

4. Use real words where they matter

Placeholder text hides problems. Write the real headline and the real button labels. To put a label inside a shape, select it and press Enter. For loose text, press T or double-click the canvas. You'll find out quickly whether "Add to cart" fits, and whether the page makes sense without the lorem ipsum.

5. Duplicate instead of redrawing

Hold Option (Alt on Windows) and drag a shape to copy it, or press Cmd + D (Ctrl + D). This also works on a whole frame, which is the fastest way to try a second version of a screen next to the first.

6. Connect the screens with arrows

Press A and drag from the button on one screen to the next frame. The arrow sticks to both ends, so it follows along when you rearrange things. Add a few words next to each arrow to say what the person did: "tap a product", "add to cart". Now your wireframe shows a flow, not just a set of pictures.

7. Tidy up

Select a few shapes and press Cmd + Shift + an arrow key to align them along that edge. Turn on the grid with Cmd + ', or snap shapes to each other with Option + S. Five minutes of tidying makes a wireframe much easier to read.

8. Reuse pieces with the library

If you draw the same navigation bar or input field on every screen, add it to your library: select it, right-click and choose Add to library. Open the Library panel in the top right to drag it onto any board. Your library is shared across all your boards and stays after you close the tab.

You can also browse the community libraries from the Library panel. There are ready-made wireframe kits there, and adding one brings its shapes straight into your library.

9. Share it

To send a picture, use Export image from the menu (Cmd + Shift + E). You can export as PNG or SVG, and if you select a single frame first, you can export just that screen. To hand over something people can edit, use Save to… to download the board file. Anyone can open it with Import board.

Want to practice?

Wireframing gets faster with reps. The Dojo gives you a random design prompt and a timer, and opens a fresh board with the brief already on it. Try a beginner prompt and give yourself 20 minutes for three screens. If you're preparing for interviews, Whiteboard Challenge Fundamentals covers how to approach a prompt before you start drawing.

Start wireframing now. It's free and there's nothing to sign up for.
