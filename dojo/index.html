How it works

  1. Pick a category and a level. Beginner prompts are a single screen or artifact. Advanced ones are full systems with competing needs.
  2. Generate a challenge. Each one tells you what to design, who it's for and who it should help. Don't like it? Generate another.
  3. Start the timer and go to the whiteboard. The board opens with the brief already on it and a timer ready to start, so you can begin sketching straight away.

What's inside

There are 30 challenges in each category, 10 per level.

  • Product & UX Design. Screens, flows and features. Example: design a collaborative playlist feature for a music streaming service, to help remote teams who want to bond.
  • Marketing & Growth. Campaigns, funnels and launch plans. Example: design a growth experiment framework for a fintech startup, to systematically test and optimize acquisition channels.
  • Sales & Pitching. Demos, pitches and objection handling. Example: design an objection handling framework for a high-ticket coaching program.
  • Strategy & Operations. Processes, plans and decisions. Example: design a vendor evaluation matrix for an operations team selecting a CRM system.
  • Data & Analytics. Reports, dashboards and experiments. Example: design an A/B test framework for an e-commerce product page.

How long to spend

  • Beginner: 15 minutes. Enough to understand the user, sketch one solution and explain it.
  • Intermediate: 20 to 30 minutes. Time to compare two directions and pick one with a reason.
  • Advanced: 30 to 45 minutes. Close to a real interview round. Spend the first few minutes on questions and constraints before you draw anything.

Use the minus and plus buttons next to the timer to change the time.

Common questions

Is the challenge generator free?

Yes. All 150 challenges, the timer and the whiteboard are free, with no account and no limits.

Do I need a challenge to use the whiteboard?

No. If you just need a place to draw, open a blank board. It works the same way, saves automatically in your browser and needs no sign-up.

How do I use it to prepare for a design interview?

Pick the level that matches the role, set the timer to the length of a real interview round and talk through your thinking out loud as you draw. Afterwards, look at what you skipped: users, constraints, success metrics.

Can I suggest a challenge?

Please do. Email hello@whiteboarddojo.com with your prompt and the level you think it fits.
